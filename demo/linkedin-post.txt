I check my portfolio a lot more on the days it's going down, which is backwards, because those are the days there's nothing to do about it anyway.

Oftentimes the checking is the whole problem. The position is the same either way, and the only thing that really changes is the mood I carry around for the rest of the day.

So I built AlwaysUp, a portfolio viewer that refuses to show a loss. Where a red day gets mirrored about the price I paid, so the loss is drawn as a gain the same size, a green day on a position that's still underwater gets lifted clear of it instead.

It also handles the thing that would make it dishonest. A badge in the header names whichever of the two it just did, it can't be dismissed, and it sits on every screen, so any screenshot carries what was done to it.

The idea is to take the sting out of checking without hiding anything. The comfort is always one gesture away from the truth, and the truth is always the thing underneath it.

Drawing the mirror was the easy part. Making sure a chart that was only moved never claims it was reflected is where most of the thinking went in this project.

The part I didn't expect to care about is the screen reader. It gets the real prices and the real loss on every screen, so the joke only ever happens in the picture. That's where the video ends.

Important to note that while the video walks through the interface, it isn't a screen recording and the prices in it are simulated. However the code is real and pulls live prices when it runs. Not investment advice, obviously.
