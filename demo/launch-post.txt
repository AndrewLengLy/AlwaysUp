2,173 characters including hashtags. First line 77 characters.

A brokerage app measures today from yesterday's close, not the opening print.

I got that detail right. Then I built the rest of the app to lie about it.

AlwaysUp is a portfolio viewer that refuses to show you a loss.

When you are underwater it mirrors the chart about the price you paid. p' = 2a - p. The anchor is a fixed point. The shape of the session is preserved exactly. The direction inverts.

So the loss comes back as a gain of exactly its own size. In the video: down $2,017.93, drawn as up $2,017.93.

Same shape. Same volatility. Green.

The premise is that most of the damage from checking your portfolio is emotional rather than financial. So it removes the emotion and leaves the rest alone.

The interesting part is what it refuses to do.

It will not call a lift a mirror. A green day on an underwater position cannot be fixed by a reflection, because the mirror would spend the day's gain to rescue the year. So it translates instead. The badge reads Lifted, and a line under the chart names the amount. $33.13, in the video.

It will not hide the badge. Mirrored, Lifted, Comfort, Honest. Non-dismissible, every screen, every mode.

It will not lie to a screen reader. Every chart carries an unmodified description of the real price action and the real profit or loss. The animated figures are aria-hidden. The joke is visual only.

It will not put an invented price under a real ticker unlabelled. Substituted data gets an amber Simulated chip, and no comfort mode removes it.

Drag the mirror track to the middle and the line goes flat. Both versions fade up either side of the axis they are mirrored about. Break-even meets the fold. The position reads exactly +0.00.

That is the whole idea in one frame.

React, TypeScript, hand-rolled SVG. No chart library: the flip is a coordinate transform, and a library would only get in the way of it. 77 tests, mostly about the maths.

The video is a reconstruction of the UI rather than a screen recording, and the market data in it is simulated. It says so on screen for all 68 seconds.

Not investment advice. Not a basis for any decision.

VARIANT C — TECHNICAL
2,592 characters including hashtags. First line 104 characters.

The 1D series is anchored to the previous close, not the open, so the overnight gap lands in the change.

That also puts the reflection's fixed point on the previous close, which is the only reason a mirrored down day inverts the whole session instead of quietly keeping what was lost before the bell.

AlwaysUp is a portfolio viewer that refuses to show you a loss.

The transform is one line. p' = 2a - p. Reflect the series about a level. The anchor is a fixed point, the shape is preserved exactly, the direction inverts.

Reflect about a level that means something — the open, or your cost basis — and the lie stays coherent: rescale the y-axis to the reflected values and every tick, high and low lands on a number that could plausibly have traded.

A property falls out of it. The reflected series spans the same vertical distance as the original, so its projection into pixels is an exact mirror, y' = plotHeight - y, whatever the anchor. The flip animation is a true reflection rather than an approximation. There is a test for it.

Outside the joke mode, every comfort is an isometry of the price axis, and a line has exactly two. A reflection inverts direction. A translation does not.

Which one is in force falls out of what needs rescuing. Down position on a down day: reflect about your cost, and one reflection fixes the day and the P&L together. Down position on a green day: a reflection would spend the day's gain, so it translates to clear your cost instead.

The app is not allowed to describe one as the other. The badge reads Lifted rather than Mirrored, and a line under the chart names the operation and the amount — $33.13 in the video.

A translation is invisible on a self-scaled axis, which is exactly why it gets a sentence instead of a shrug.

In pixel space the fold is the plot's horizontal midline, whatever the anchor was. Park the mirror track at 50% and the line goes flat, both versions fade up either side of it, break-even meets the fold, and the position reads exactly +0.00. That axis is only drawn when the transform genuinely is a reflection.

Screen readers get the real numbers. The animated figures are aria-hidden with a static truthful summary beside them.

React, TypeScript, hand-rolled SVG, Framer Motion. No chart library. 77 tests.

The video is a reconstruction of the UI rather than a screen recording, and the market data in it is the project's own simulator on a frozen clock. It says so on screen for all 68 seconds.

Not investment advice. Not a basis for any decision.
